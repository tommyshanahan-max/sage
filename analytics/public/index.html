                  One person means one browser. Counting is a random id this site puts in that browser's own storage — no account, no IP address kept, no third party, nothing that follows anyone anywhere else. The same person on a phone and a laptop is two; someone who clears their storage is new again.

                  Two things are recorded about arrival, both once per person per day and both coarse: the browser's own timezone, and the host that linked here. The timezone is asked of the browser, not derived from a network address — the address is never read, which is why the line above stays true and why no database of anybody's addresses exists on this machine. Neither is kept against a person: they are day totals, the same as everything else here.

                  Days are not added up. The bars are people per day, and the same person on Monday and Tuesday is one person, not two — so there is no honest weekly total to show and none is shown. Pages and functions are summed over the range, because those are actions, and two of them are two.

                  Came back counts people who were here today and had been before. The percentage beside the growth curve is a different question — of everyone ever counted, how many have returned on any later day. Both come from the same two dates already kept per browser, so neither needed anything new recorded.

                  Average visit is how long the page was actually on screen — time in a background tab does not count, and anything past half an hour is dropped as a tab left open rather than counted as a very long visit. It is kept as a running total and a count, never as one person's visit.

                  Nothing here is finer than a day. The browser's id is hashed before it touches disk, and no time of day is written next to it — so these files record how many people came, never when any one of them was here.
