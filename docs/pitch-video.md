# Ninety seconds, for an investor

A script and a shot list. Read the caveats at the bottom before you record
anything — two of the numbers in here need checking and one of them is the one
you would most regret getting wrong.

---

## The script

Roughly 225 words, which is ninety seconds at a normal speaking pace. Timings
are cumulative.

**[0:00 — 0:12]**

> Duolingo is worth billions of dollars. Over a hundred million people open it
> every month.
>
> And almost none of them can order dinner.

**[0:12 — 0:30]**

> Language apps teach you a language. Slowly, gamified, over years. That is a
> real business and it is enormous.
>
> But it is not the same business as helping somebody who is standing in a shop
> in Beijing, right now, holding a packet they cannot read.

**[0:30 — 0:50]**

> That is Study Pal. You press one button and speak English. It says the
> Chinese out loud. The other person answers, and it comes back in English.
>
> Point the camera at a menu and it tells you what is in it. Ask it where the
> nearest pharmacy is and it answers in the language of the person behind the
> counter.
>
> No account. No sign-up. And — this is the part nobody else does — no VPN. It
> loads inside the mainland, which is most of the reason it exists.

**[0:50 — 1:10]**

> There are roughly a million foreigners living in mainland China, and millions
> more visiting each year. Almost none of them speak enough Chinese. Every one
> of them has the same bad afternoon in their first week.
>
> They are not trying to become fluent. They are trying to get through today.

**[1:10 — 1:30]**

> It is live. It works. People are using it without us telling anybody it
> exists.
>
> Duolingo won the market for wanting to learn a language. We want the market
> for needing one this afternoon — and China is where that need is sharpest and
> the competition cannot follow.

## What still has to happen

Seedance makes clip 1 through 11. Everything else is yours:

- **Voiceover.** ElevenLabs is already configured for Study Pal, so the voice
  is available. Or record it yourself — a real voice with a slight hesitation
  outperforms a synthetic one in a room with three people in it.
- **Titles.** At minimum the name and the URL at the end.
- **The edit.** Any editor. The cuts should land on the beats above.
- **Music.** Quiet, and under. Licence it properly; an investor deck is a
  commercial use.

## Check these before you say them out loud

**"Worth billions" and "over a hundred million monthly."** Both are the right
order of magnitude and both move. Look up the current market cap and the latest
reported MAU before you record. A specific wrong number in front of an investor
costs more than a vague right one — if you cannot check, say "worth billions"
and "tens of millions of users a month" and stay safe.

**"Roughly a million foreigners in mainland China."** This is the number I am
least sure of, and it is the one your whole market sizing rests on. The last
census put permanent foreign residents well under a million; the figure people
quote in pitches usually includes short-stay visitors and students, which is a
different claim. Find the source you would be willing to be challenged on, and
use that number instead of this one.

**"People are using it without us telling anybody."** True, and it is your best
line — but only say it with the real figure beside it. As of the first week of
September the counter reads 126 people ever, about 20 new in a day, and 849
uses. That is small, and saying it plainly is stronger than implying more: a
hundred and twenty-six people in ten days with no marketing is a rate, and a
rate is what gets funded.
