The Exchange·design for the room

The room ledger, pinned to the top of the chat

Every person in a room sees their own line: the board's illustrative sale figure, worked out from how many people are active in it, multiplied down to their share. When the room grows, everybody's figure grows with it. It uses the points the board already keeps; the only new number is a figure per member.

How it works, and what it must not do

The board already has most of this. stakeOf() in board/server.js scores every member (an early seat, guests who stay, introductions, people who answered, active weeks), and the waiting room turns those points into dollars from one fixed sale figure. This design makes that sale figure grow with the room and moves it from the waiting room to the top of the chat.

The formula

sale = active members × per-member figure

yours = sale × members' share × your points ÷ founding pool

The founding pool is every point the first hundred seats can hold (3,717 at today's weights), not today's members. That keeps anyone's share from shrinking as the hundred fill, which is the reason the code already divides this way.

Who counts as active

A member whose page is up and who has posted in the last 30 days. Not everyone who ever joined: a head count that includes people who left is the easiest number here to inflate, and it's what a buyer would discount first.

The pin shows the count it used, so nobody has to trust it.

Rules it keeps

  • No default figure. Until the board sets one, the pin shows points and a percentage, and no dollar sign appears.
  • Only you see your line. The room shares one count; nobody sees anyone else's figure. No ranking.
  • It says what it is, every time: an illustration, not a valuation or an offer.
  • It stops on the closing date, like the ledger already does.

With a screen reader

The pin is one button whose label is a whole sentence: "Your ledger: $169 if the board sold at today's size. Expand to see how it's worked out."

When someone joins, it waits a moment and says once: "The room is now 413 members. Your illustration is about $171." It never announces every tick, and it never interrupts.

Before the dollar figure goes live

A number that tells a group of people what their share of a sale would be, and that grows when they bring others in, is the shape regulators read as offering an investment. Securities rules in Hong Kong, the US and mainland China can all reach it, and "recruit to raise your number" can read as a pyramid even when it isn't one.

I'm not a lawyer. Have one read the exact wording before a per-member figure is set on a live board. Points mode shows no money and can ship first.

Where it goes in the code

  • board/server.js: a new BOARD_STAKE_PER_MEMBER replaces the fixed BOARD_STAKE_SALE; the sale is worked out per request from the active count. BOARD_STAKE_CUT stays.
  • The room data gains a ledger object for the person asking, and only them.
  • board/public/room.html: a sticky pin under the top bar, closed by default, updated from the arrival event Mo already announces.
  • board/public/i18n.js: the pin's lines in English and Chinese.

The share offers in cfm/ (percentages with vesting) are a separate record and aren't part of this number.

Decisions that are yours

  1. The figure per active member. Well-known acquisitions have worked out at tens of dollars per monthly user (WhatsApp, 2014, about $42), but a small invite-only club usually sells on revenue, not heads.
  2. Whether 30 days is the right window for "active".
  3. What people who join after the first hundred get. Today the ledger gives them no share, so their pin says so plainly. The alternative is a second, smaller tier.
  4. The members' share of a sale, and the exact words for what happens if one takes place.
