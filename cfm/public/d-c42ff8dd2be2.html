Before you say yes · 1

This slice gets thinner.

What you are offered is a share of the company as it is today. Raising money makes new shares, and everybody already holding some ends up with a smaller piece of a bigger thing.

A round that sells a fifth of the company takes a fifth off your slice. Two rounds and it is a third gone. None of the figures on the last slide account for that, and no honest version of them could — it depends on money nobody has raised yet.

Before you say yes · 2

It is worth nothing until somebody buys it.

These are paper figures on a private company. There is no market for the shares, no dividend, and no date.

They turn into money if the company sells, or raises at a price. If it does neither, a share of it is a share of nothing. That is the actual shape of the bet, and it is worth saying out loud before you take it.
