Nothing Chinese owns anything and nothing Chinese is owed anything. That is the whole trick.

The one rule

The money never lands in China.

A licence is needed to run a paid internet service inside China. Charge in dollars to a Singapore account and we are a foreign company somebody chose to buy from.

Chinese users can still pay with Alipay or WeChat Pay — Stripe takes it in RMB and settles to Singapore in dollars. Same as Steam, same as Airbnb.

What we never do: take RMB into a Chinese bank account. That is the line, and it is one line.

For the next year

What we do, and what we leave alone.

  • Charge in dollars to the Singapore company, by card or by Alipay through Stripe.
  • Ship to the App Store — every storefront except mainland China. Most foreigners here have a foreign Apple ID anyway.
  • Keep the servers in Tokyo. Fast enough, and it keeps the whole question theoretical.
  • No Chinese company, no ICP licence, no Chinese bank account.
  • No mainland App Store. It needs a Chinese entity and a filing, and it is the one thing that puts us on a list.
  • No shares for numbers. Equity is a fixed grant for a named role. Never more shares for bringing more people.

When we pivot

Only when the money is Chinese.

  1. The trigger Chinese nationals paying in RMB become most of the revenue. Not user numbers — revenue.
  2. What it costs A Chinese company, an ICP licence, real-name sign-up, and a compliance job that has to be somebody's. Months, not weeks.
  3. What we do first A thousand foreigners in China using it every week. That number answers the question on its own.

Written as a plan, not as advice. Every threshold here is real, but a China lawyer signs it off before the first real revenue — an hour of their time, not a project.
